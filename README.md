# AI for Small Business — a Plain-English Guide

A free, no-hype guide to **using AI in a small business**: what actually saves time, what to avoid, and a 30-day plan to get your first win. Written for owners and office managers, not engineers.

Maintained by [Korra Academy](https://korra.academy/) — the team behind **[Korra.Business](https://korra.business/)**, AI training and automation help for small firms.

## The one-paragraph answer

Start with AI as a **drafting and summarising assistant** for work you already do — replies, quotes, job ads, meeting notes, product descriptions. That is where small businesses reliably save 3–5 hours a week with near-zero risk. Automations and "AI agents" come later, once a human-reviewed workflow already works.

## Where AI pays off first (ranked by effort-to-value)

1. **Customer replies & quotes** — paste the enquiry, ask for a draft in your tone, edit, send. Never send unread.
2. **Meeting & call notes** — record (with consent), get a summary with action points.
3. **Marketing copy** — product descriptions, social posts, email newsletters; AI drafts, you approve.
4. **Documents & SOPs** — turn a rambling voice note into a checklist your staff can follow.
5. **Spreadsheet help** — "write the formula that…" ends hours of Googling.
6. **Website & SEO basics** — page copy drafts, FAQ generation (pair with our [small-business website advice](https://korra.business/)).
7. **Simple chatbots for FAQs** — only after your FAQ page itself is good (see [build-your-first-chatbot](https://github.com/korra-academy/build-your-first-chatbot) to understand what a bot really is).
8. **Full automations / AI agents** — invoice chasing, booking flows, triage. Genuinely valuable, but do these LAST and keep a human check on anything customer-facing.

## The 30-day starter plan

**Week 1 — Pick one job.** One repetitive writing task (e.g. quote emails). Ten minutes a day: do it with AI alongside your normal way.
**Week 2 — Build the prompt.** Save the instruction that works ("You write quotes for a plumbing firm; tone friendly, always include call-out fee…"). That saved prompt is your first business asset.
**Week 3 — Roll to the team.** Share the prompt, agree the rule: *AI drafts, humans send.*
**Week 4 — Measure and pick the next job.** Count hours saved. Repeat with task #2.

## Rules that keep you safe

- **Never paste customer personal data** (names + addresses, health, finances) into consumer AI tools. Use business tiers with data controls, or anonymise first. Check your GDPR obligations.
- **A human reviews anything a customer sees.** AI is confidently wrong sometimes; your reputation isn't the place to discover that.
- **Don't buy tools yet.** The chat assistant you already have covers the first three months. Buy software only when a specific bottleneck demands it.
- **Write down where AI is used** — a one-page AI policy stops staff pasting payroll into random websites.

## FAQ

**What is the best AI tool for a small business?**
For starting out, any major chat assistant (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini) on a paid business plan. The differentiator is your prompts and process, not the tool.

**Will AI replace my staff?**
In small firms it mostly removes the boring 20% of existing jobs — admin, drafting, note-taking. The win is capacity, not headcount.

**What's an "AI agent"?**
Software that uses AI to complete multi-step tasks (read email → draft reply → update spreadsheet) rather than answering one question. Powerful, but start with assistants first.

**How much should a small business budget for AI?**
£20–£60 per user per month covers assistant tools. Bespoke automation is a project cost — scope it only after the free wins are banked.
